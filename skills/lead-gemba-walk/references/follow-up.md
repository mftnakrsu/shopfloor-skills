# Follow-up

How to turn walk observations into closed findings, how to write them without blame, and how to measure the walk system.

## Writing an observation

Write what a camera would have seen, where and how often, plus the person's own explanation in their words. Label any interpretation.

| Instead of (judges a person) | Write (describes the process) |
|---|---|
| "Operator careless, did not follow the standard" | "Step 5 done in a different order from the work instruction on 2 of 4 cycles watched; operator says the fixture only fits that way" |
| "Nobody at the press" | "Press unmanned for about 15 min; its operator was covering a second machine during break relief" |
| "Team ignores the board" | "Board actuals last updated 3 shifts ago; team says nobody reacts to the reds" |

(Invented examples.)

For each standard not followed, ask in this order before writing a cause (rule of thumb, from the respect rules in Shook and KaiNexus):

1. Is there a standard, and does the person know it?
2. Is it clear and possible to follow in the time given?
3. Are the means there: material, tools, protective equipment, information?
4. Was the person trained and checked on it?
5. What else got in the way?

Only if all five are yes is it a question of conduct, and that goes to the line manager outside the walk, never into the walk log.

## From observation to finding

- Safety or product-quality risk: contain at once (stop, protect, segregate), log as contained, then treat it as a finding.
- Gap between standard and work, waste, recurring disturbance, broken management routine: a finding.
- Good practice worth spreading: log it too, and tell the team.
- Pick the few findings that matter most for the walk's purpose; the rest still get an owner.

## Routing a finding

| Finding | Route |
|---|---|
| Cause known, simple fix | Action on the area board, followed in the tier meeting |
| Cause unknown | Structured problem solving outside the walk (for example an A3 or an 8D) with the people who do the work |
| Equipment loss | Loss tree, then maintenance or set-up improvement |
| Waiting, piles of work in process | Flow analysis of throughput time and work in process |
| Standard inadequate | Revise the standard with the team, then train and confirm it |

Every route gets an owner, a due date and a verification step (KaiNexus).

## Closing the loop

- Tell the people at the gemba what was learned and what will happen next, even when nothing happens at once (KaiNexus).
- Return to the gemba to verify that the change worked, by watching the work and by the KPI (KaiNexus).
- Close a finding only after that verification (rule of thumb, following KaiNexus).
- Open the next walk in the area with the open findings from the last one (rule of thumb).

## Walk metrics

Measure over a fixed period; the period and targets are set by local policy.

| Metric | Formula | Worked example (invented round numbers) |
|---|---|---|
| Walks held % | walks held ÷ walks planned × 100 | 9 ÷ 12 × 100 = 75% |
| Findings closed on time % | findings verified closed by their due date ÷ findings due × 100 | 21 ÷ 28 × 100 = 75% |
| Average open age (days) | sum of the ages of open findings ÷ number of open findings | (3 + 5 + 7 + 15) ÷ 4 = 7.5 days |
| Team told back % | findings whose next step was told to the team ÷ findings logged × 100 | 27 ÷ 30 × 100 = 90% |

A finding marked "done" but not yet verified counts as open.
