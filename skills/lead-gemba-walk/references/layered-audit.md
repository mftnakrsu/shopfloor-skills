# Layered audit segment

How to build and run the optional layered process audit (LPA) segment inside one walk. Designing the LPA programme (layers, frequencies, checklist rules, schedule, metrics and the review of results) is `audit-process`.

## Contents

- Running the segment
- IATF 16949 pointers

## What the segment is

- An LPA is a short, scheduled yes/no check of process inputs and controls, done where the work is done, by several layers of management, each at its own frequency. The guideline most used in automotive is AIAG CQI-8 (2nd edition 2014, a paid document; summarised here from public secondary sources).
- It checks process inputs and controls, not finished-product characteristics, and not paperwork for its own sake (EASE).
- The walker's layer and how often the walker audits this area come from the plant's LPA programme or the customer's requirements; ask for them.
- A full LPA takes about 10 minutes (EASE) or 10–15 minutes (Tervene).
- Walkers do their own segment. One supplier procedure accepts delegation only in extenuating circumstances (Nexteer).

## Building the items

- Take the items from the control points the user gives (control plan, error-proofing list, standard work), keeping their wording and interval.
- Usual categories to confirm with the user (Nexteer, paraphrased): error-proofing verification records checked and the devices work; standard work followed; operator trained or certified for the job; control-plan checks at the right frequency, sample size and form; reaction plans followed when a result is outside its limits.
- Keep the segment short: 5–10 items inside a gemba walk (rule of thumb), so the walk keeps time for open observation.
- Write each item so that the answer is seen, not told: "the last check entry is within its interval", not "do you do the check?" (rule of thumb).

## Running the segment

1. Audit during real work, not when the line is empty (Tervene).
2. Review the records, watch the work, and ask the operator without stopping production (Nexteer).
3. Answer each item Yes or No. Mark what you could not check "not reviewed" (Nexteer).
4. Correct a No at once if it is within your authority, and write down the action and the date. Pass the others to the responsible person and note who was told (Nexteer).
5. If product quality is at risk, the area acts at once. What cannot be corrected at once goes into the corrective-action process (Nexteer).
6. Record a No as a No even when it was corrected during the audit; the programme counts what was conforming when checked (Nexteer).
7. Hand the filled checklist to the LPA programme owner; its results and metrics are reviewed there (`audit-process`).

## IATF 16949 pointers

As of 2026-11-25: IATF 16949:2016 with its sanctioned interpretations. Summaries are in our own words; use the official text and the customer-specific requirements for the binding wording.

- **9.2.2.3 Manufacturing process audit.** The standard does not name LPAs, but several automotive customers require them in their specific requirements for this clause (EASE). An LPA feeds the process audit; it does not replace it.
- **8.5.6.1.1 Temporary change of process controls.** Where an alternate control method is in use, its use is reviewed daily. An LPA item, or the tier meeting, can carry that daily review.
