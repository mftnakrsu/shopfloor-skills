# The A3 sheet

How to write and review one A3 so that it carries a PDCA cycle.

## Sections and the PDCA step they hold

| Section | PDCA | Question it answers | Writing rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title, owner, date | | What is this about; who owns it; latest revision | The title names the problem, not the solution |
| Background | Plan | Why does it matter? | One or two lines on the effect on safety, quality, delivery or cost |
| Current condition | Plan | Where do things stand, measured? | Baseline value, how counted, period, shifts; a chart or sketch |
| Goal | Plan | What result, by when? | A number and a date, and who set it |
| Analysis | Plan | What causes the gap? | The simplest tool that shows cause and effect; mark each cause proven or hypothesis |
| Countermeasures | Plan | What acts on which cause? | One line per countermeasure with the cause it acts on and its prediction |
| Plan | Do | Who does what, when; which indicator? | Owner and date per action; the test plan |
| Check | Check | What happened, against prediction and goal? | Measured result, change, gap closed, what is left, limits |
| Follow-up | Act | Adopt, adapt or abandon; what next; what was learned? | The new standard and its check, or the next cycle |

The section list follows Shook and Verble's template; the Check row is drawn out as its own box here so that it cannot be skipped. The layout can change; every question must still be answered (Shook).

## Reviewing an A3

The reviewer (manager or mentor) coaches by asking, and the owner keeps the problem (LEI). Ask in this order and stop at the first weak answer:

1. Is the problem stated as a measured gap, not as a solution?
2. Did the owner go and see? Which facts come from the floor?
3. Is the cause proven, and how?
4. Does each countermeasure name the cause it acts on?
5. How will we know it worked: which number, counted how, predicted at what?
6. Who has agreed: the area, the other shifts, the functions affected?
7. What can go wrong in the test, and how is it watched?
8. What did the check show, and does the act decision follow from it?
9. How will the learning be shared?

These follow the ten guiding questions in Shook, in this skill's words.

## Keeping the sheet honest

- One sheet per topic; each cycle adds its prediction, result and act decision, dated. Do not overwrite an earlier cycle.
- Facts carry their source (log, count, observation). Opinions are marked as such.
- A section that cannot be filled stays open with the question it needs, an owner and a date.
- The A3 closes when the goal is met in a measured check and the new standard is released and checked; not when the actions are done.
