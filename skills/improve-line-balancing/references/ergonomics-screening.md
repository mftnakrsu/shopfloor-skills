# Ergonomics screening

Public methods to screen work elements before they are loaded onto operators, and how each result constrains the balance. Screening does not replace an assessment by a qualified ergonomist where local policy requires one.

## Choose the method

| Work | Method |
|---|---|
| Lifting or lowering a load with both hands | Revised NIOSH lifting equation (NIOSH) |
| Repetitive or static work of the arms, wrists, neck and trunk, mostly standing or seated in place | RULA (McAtamney & Corlett) |
| Whole-body work with walking, bending, lifting or rapid posture changes | REBA (Hignett & McAtamney) |

The RULA author's notes advise REBA where the task involves walking, bending, lifting or rapid posture changes (McAtamney).

## Revised NIOSH lifting equation

Recommended weight limit RWL = LC × HM × VM × DM × AM × FM × CM (NIOSH; course notes after Lehto & Landry).

| Factor | Metric formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| LC, load constant | 23 kg | Ideal lift under best conditions |
| HM, horizontal | 25 ÷ H, H from 25 to 63 cm | Hand distance from the ankles' midpoint |
| VM, vertical | 1 − 0.003 × \|V − 75\|, V from 0 to 175 cm | Hand height at the start of the lift |
| DM, distance | 0.82 + 4.5 ÷ D, D from 25 to 175 cm | Vertical travel of the load |
| AM, asymmetry | 1 − 0.0032 × A, A from 0 to 135° | Trunk twist |
| FM, frequency | From the table in the NIOSH applications manual | Lifts per minute and task duration |
| CM, coupling | From the table in the NIOSH applications manual | Grip quality: good, fair or poor |

Lifting index LI = load ÷ RWL.

- NIOSH recommends LI at or below 1.0. For several lifting tasks, use the composite lifting index (CLI) (NIOSH).
- LI above 1.0 means an increased risk of lifting-related low back pain for part of the workforce; LI above 3.0 marks a highly stressful task (course notes after Lehto & Landry).
- Example (invented): 10 kg lifted with H = 50 cm, V = 25 cm, D = 50 cm, A = 0°, FM = 0.75 and CM = 0.95 from the manual tables. HM = 0.50, VM = 0.85, DM = 0.91, AM = 1.00. RWL = 23 × 0.50 × 0.85 × 0.91 × 1.00 × 0.75 × 0.95 ≈ 6.3 kg. LI = 10 ÷ 6.3 ≈ 1.6: redesign.
- The usual levers follow the formula: bring the load closer (H), lift from waist height (V near 75 cm), shorten the travel (D), remove the twist (A), lift less often or for shorter periods (FM), add handles (CM).

## RULA

The grand score runs from 1 to 7 (McAtamney).

| Score | Action level | Action (Cornell summary) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 1 | Acceptable posture |
| 3–4 | 2 | Some risk: investigate and correct |
| 5–6 | 3 | Poor posture: investigate and change in the near future |
| 7 | 4 | Worst posture: investigate and change immediately |

## REBA

- Scores upper arm, lower arm, wrist, neck, trunk and legs, with load and activity; the final score runs from 1 to 15 (ehs.com summary).
- A score of 8 or more indicates high risk, and engineering controls are recommended (ehs.com summary).
- Assess before and after a change to show that it lowered the risk (Cornell).

## How the result constrains the balance

These limits are rules of thumb for the balance, built on the thresholds above:

1. LI above 1.0, RULA 5 or more, or REBA 8 or more: the element is a high-risk element. Raise a redesign action with an owner and date before or with the new balance.
2. Do not place two high-risk elements on the same operator.
3. Do not use fill-up to add lifts or repetitions of the same high-risk posture to one operator. Higher frequency lowers FM and raises LI.
4. Until the fix is in, plan job rotation between stations with different loads (rotation interval set by local policy).
5. A shorter takt raises the lift frequency of every element done once per unit. Re-check LI when takt drops.
6. Re-assess after the fix and record both scores.
