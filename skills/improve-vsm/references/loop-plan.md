# Loop plan

How to turn the future-state map into loops with owners, targets, dates and 30/60/90-day reviews.

## Loops

Split the future-state map into loops (Teamguru):

1. **Pacemaker loop:** the pacemaker, the flow after it to shipping, the finished-goods supermarket or shipping link, levelling and pitch. It goes first: it sets the rhythm the other loops follow.
2. **One loop per upstream supermarket or FIFO lane:** the supplying process, its bursts (C/O, uptime, %C/A) and its signal.
3. **Supplier loop:** delivery frequency, pack size and the signal to the supplier.

Run the loops in sequence, with overlap only where different people do the work. Launching all loops at once overloads the same people (Teamguru).

## What each loop needs

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Kaizen bursts | Listed from the map, each with its gap in numbers |
| Owner | One named role; for the whole stream, a value stream manager who owns the current state, the future state and the plan across departments (Think Insights) |
| Target | In numbers: lead time days, inventory days at named points, C/O minutes, uptime %, %C/A, pitch hits per shift |
| Dates | Start and end; the first loop starts within 2 to 3 weeks of mapping (LEI eBook, Worth) |
| Review | 30-, 60- and 90-day reviews, then monthly in the management review (LEI eBook, Worth; Teamguru) |

## Horizon

- Sources differ: Teamguru dates the future state 6 to 12 months out; LEI (Worth) nests 30-, 60- and 90-day plans inside a future state 18 to 24 months out. Agree the horizon with the user and write it on the plan.
- An undated future state does not get resources (Teamguru).

## 30/60/90-day review

At each review:

1. Compare each loop's target with the measured value; mark it green or red.
2. Re-count the inventory points of the loop and rebuild its part of the ladder.
3. For a red target, name the cause and the next experiment; the future state is a hypothesis tested by learning cycles (LEI eBook).
4. Confirm the next loop's start date and owner.
5. At 90 days, redraw the current-state map for the finished loops.

A late loop is a management problem, not a reason to redraw the future state. Redraw it when it is reached, or when demand or mix moves takt (Teamguru).

## Worked example (invented)

| Loop | Bursts | Owner | Target | Start | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pacemaker | Pitch board, levelling box, finished-goods supermarket 2 days | Assembly supervisor | Finished goods from 5 to 2 days; pitch hits ≥ 95% of pitches (set by local policy) | week 2 | week 10 |
| Moulding supermarket | C/O 50 → 20 min, kanban cards | Moulding team leader | EPEI 6 → 3 days; supermarket 3 days | week 8 | week 20 |
| Supplier | Daily delivery | Logistics planner | Raw stock 8 → 3 days | week 16 | week 30 |

Reviews at day 30, 60 and 90, then monthly. Future lead time = sum of the designed inventory days, stated as the target once all bursts are done.
