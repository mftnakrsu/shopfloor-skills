# Autonomous-maintenance steps and exit criteria

The seven steps in JIPM order, what each step must deliver, and the exit criteria to check in a step audit.

## Step order and phases

The step order is defined by JIPM and taught in seven steps in three phases (Industry Forum, an associate agency of JIPM; Nakajima's chapter on AM in seven steps). Japanese names from a JIPM-linked consultant (MONOist). Some web pages put general inspection at step 5; use the JIPM order below.

| Step | Name | Japanese | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (optional) | Safety and equipment functions | — | Preparation |
| 1 | Initial cleaning (cleaning is inspection) | 初期清掃 | 1: restore and prevent deterioration |
| 2 | Countermeasures for contamination sources and hard-to-access areas | 発生源・困難箇所対策 | 1 |
| 3 | Provisional cleaning, lubrication and inspection standards | 自主保全仮基準の作成 | 1 |
| 4 | General inspection | 総点検 | 2: build inspection skill |
| 5 | Autonomous inspection | 自主点検 | 2 |
| 6 | Standardisation (workplace organisation) | 標準化 | 3: team autonomy |
| 7 | Autonomous management | 自主管理 | 3 |

Steps 1 to 3 restore the machine and stop it from deteriorating again. Steps 4 to 7 build the operators' skill and the team's ownership (Industry Forum).

## Abnormality tags

Seven types of abnormality are tagged in step 1 (Reliability magazine guide):

1. Minor defects (loose, worn, cracked, leaking parts)
2. Unfulfilled basic conditions (cleaning, lubrication, tightening)
3. Inaccessible places (hard to clean, inspect or lubricate)
4. Contamination sources
5. Quality defect sources
6. Unnecessary and non-urgent items
7. Unsafe places

Each tag records number, date, type, location, finder, whether operators or maintenance fix it, owner, due date and closing date. Count hung, closed and open tags by type and by fixer.

## Exit criteria per step

No public source gives numeric exit criteria for each step. The criteria below are built from the step deliverables in the sources. Every target is set by local policy unless a source is named.

| Step | Exit criteria |
|---|---|
| 0 | Lock-out and safety rules for the machine are known and applied by every team member; the main functions and units of the machine are explained on a sketch |
| 1 | The machine is cleaned to the base material; abnormalities are tagged by type; every open tag has an owner and a due date; tags are closed within about eight weeks (rule of thumb, Industry Forum); contamination sources and hard-to-access areas are listed; a first cleaning standard exists |
| 2 | Every contamination source has a countermeasure at the source, or a dated plan; every hard-to-access area is improved; the same dirt no longer returns between cleanings; cleaning time per shift is measured before and after and meets the target |
| 3 | A posted standard for each item to clean, lubricate and check: method, tool, frequency, time, who (operator or maintenance); visual controls (gauge ranges, lubrication points and oil types marked, numbered route); the team completes it within the time budget; adherence records exist |
| 4 | Operators have been trained by unit (for example lubrication, fasteners, hydraulics, pneumatics, electrics, drives) with one-point lessons; for each unit they found and fixed or tagged the hidden defects; the inspection items are added to the standard |
| 5 | Provisional standards are merged into final standards; operator and maintenance tasks are split with no gap and no overlap; checks are made easy and error-proofed; checks are done on time to standard |
| 6 | Standards extend beyond the machine: workplace organisation, material, tools, quality checks and records; the standards are kept up to date by the team |
| 7 | The team sets its own goals from the plant's policy, tracks its losses (for example MTBF, minor stops, defects), and runs improvement cycles without being pushed |

Signs that step 2 was skipped: the same dirt or leak returns every shift, cleaning time stays high, and contamination tags stay open. Without step 2 the team is stuck in an endless cycle of cleaning (Industry Forum; Gemba Academy). Starting from the end point, the posted standard, without restoration and improvement is the most common mistake (Industry Forum).

## Running a step audit

1. Audit at the machine, during or right after a cleaning, with the team present.
2. Self-audit by the team, then the area manager, then the TPM coordinator before the team moves on (Reliability magazine guide).
3. Re-check the criteria of all earlier steps; a later step cannot pass on top of a failed earlier one.
4. Score each criterion met or not met, with the evidence seen. No partial pass.
5. For each gap: the fix, who does it (operators or maintenance), the support maintenance or management must give, and a date.
6. Set a re-audit date. Pilot one machine in depth before rolling out (Industry Forum).
