# Operator instruction writing

How to write the station document so that operators can follow it. These are practice rules, not standards, except where a clause is named.

## Step breakdown (TWI Job Instruction)

- Important step: what advances the job. Start with a verb: "Place", "Press", "Measure".
- Key points: anything that makes or breaks quality, could injure the worker, or makes the work easier (the knack). Safety is always a key point.
- Reason: one short reason for each key point. Writing reasons forces the team to agree on one best way.
- More than about five key points on one step: split the step (rule of thumb).

## Layout

1. Header: document ID, revision, control-plan and PFMEA reference with revision, part or family, operation, approvals.
2. Safety and PPE strip at the top (IATF 16949 8.5.1.2).
3. Numbered step rows: picture or pictogram, step, key points, reasons, special-characteristic symbol and control-plan characteristic number where they apply.
4. Check rows: what, gauge ID, method, how many and how often, OK if, where to record, OK/NOK picture pair or limit-sample ID.
5. Reaction box: stop, mark and segregate, contain back to the last good check, call the named role, record.
6. Footer: change history, training level required.

## Pictures and text

- Combined pictures and short text tend to give faster work and fewer errors than text alone (cluster of studies; effects vary with the task).
- Put numbers and units next to the picture. Keep sentences short; avoid negations.
- OK/NOK pairs show both sides of the boundary and name the physical limit sample, its ID and approval. For appearance checks state lighting and viewing distance (8.6.3).

## One-point lessons

- One page, one point, mostly a picture, taught in a few minutes. Use one for a single knack or a recurring mistake. If the point needs "and", make two.

## Language and access (8.5.1.2)

- Understood by the people doing the work, legible, in their languages, at the work area.
- Record which languages the station's operators read. A single-language document on a mixed-language team is a finding.
- Pictograms reduce the text that needs translation; the check values and reaction still need every language.

## Operator self-inspection (Werkerselbstprüfung)

- When the operator checks their own work, the instruction names the operator's authority to stop the process and the exact escalation path.
- Quality planning still owns the inspection plan, gauge management and audits.

## Training and release

- Train before first use, with on-the-job training matched to the task and the person (7.2.2), and record it. Retrain after every revision that changes a step, a check or a reaction.
