# Shopfloor-management self-audit

How to audit shopfloor management (Shopfloor-Management, SFM) across areas: the five audit areas, the scoring, and how to report the result. Fixing a routine that the audit finds weak is done with the daily-routine skill.

## Audit areas

Five areas with yes/no questions, observed in real meetings (ZielKom report; questions paraphrased and grouped):

| Area | Ask, and check on evidence |
|---|---|
| Performance management | KPIs are defined with targets; actuals are compared with the target every day; each deviation is recorded and passed into problem solving |
| Meeting process | An agenda exists and is on the board; the meeting follows it; it stays within its timebox; it ends with decisions and owners |
| Problem solving | Problems are started, tracked and worked with a structured method; root causes are found; countermeasures are done as agreed, checked and standardised |
| Visual management | The board shows the current production status, the agenda, the KPI profile and the problem solving in progress, all up to date |
| Competence development | Team members take part in problem solving and in carrying out the actions; this is linked to how their skills are developed |

## Running the audit

1. Observe real meetings on real days, at every tier in the scope, not a meeting staged for the audit (ZielKom report).
2. The auditor comes from outside the area (independence; ISO 19011 principle).
3. Answer each question Yes or No on what you saw; "not observed" when the meeting gave no chance to see it.
4. Check one red KPI from the board through to its action and its problem solving: is there an owner, a date and progress?
5. Record each No as a finding with its evidence, the same way as in a process audit.

## Scoring

- Area score % = Yes ÷ (Yes + No) × 100. "Not observed" items are left out.
- Worked example (invented round numbers): problem solving, 8 questions over 2 meetings = 16 answers, 2 not observed, 7 Yes, 7 No → 7 ÷ 14 × 100 = 50%.
- Report each area separately; do not average the five into one number that hides the weak area (rule of thumb).
- Targets per area are set by local policy.

## What audits usually find

- In audits at three German manufacturers, the meeting process and visual management were mostly strong, while problem solving and competence development were weak or missing (ZielKom report). Expect the gap there, and look hardest at whether a red KPI ever becomes a solved problem.
- Low participation of the team in the meeting was a common moderation gap (ZielKom report).

## Clause pointers

Shopfloor management is not a named requirement in ISO 9001 or IATF 16949. Findings map to the clause behind the gap, for example monitoring and analysis (ISO 9001 9.1.1, 9.1.3; IATF 16949 9.1.1.1), corrective action and problem solving (ISO 9001 10.2; IATF 16949 10.2.3) or continual improvement (ISO 9001 10.3; IATF 16949 10.3.1). When no clause fits, the criterion is the local SFM standard.
