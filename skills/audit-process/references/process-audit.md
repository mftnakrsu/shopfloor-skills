# Manufacturing process audit

How to plan, run and score a manufacturing process audit, with the IATF 16949 requirements and the VDA 6.3 scoring method.

## Contents

## What the standard asks for

As checked in November 2026: IATF 16949:2016 is the auditable edition. Summaries in our own words; use the official text and the customer-specific requirements for the binding wording.

- **9.2.2.1 Internal audit programme.** A documented audit process whose programme covers quality-management-system audits, manufacturing process audits and product audits. The programme is set by risk, internal and external performance trends and how critical each process is. Audit frequency is reviewed and raised after process changes, nonconformities or customer complaints. The programme's effectiveness is reviewed in management review (DNV matrix).
- **9.2.2.3 Manufacturing process audit.** Every manufacturing process is audited over each three-year calendar period for effectiveness and efficiency, with the approach the customer specifies, or one the organisation chooses where the customer specifies none. Each audit covers all shifts where the process runs, including a sample of the shift handover, and checks that the PFMEA, the control plan and their associated documents are effectively implemented (DNV matrix).
- **7.2.3 Internal auditor competency.** A documented way to verify auditor competence and a list of qualified auditors. Process auditors understand the automotive process approach with risk-based thinking, the customer-specific requirements, the ISO 9001 and IATF 16949 requirements and core tools in scope, how to plan, run, report and close findings, and the process itself with its PFMEA and control plan. Competence is kept up by a minimum number of audits per year set by the organisation (DNV matrix).
- **ISO 19011:2018** gives the general method (principles, audit programme, conducting audits, auditor competence). The 2023 edition of VDA 6.3 removed its own audit-process chapter because ISO 19011 covers it (DQS).

## Planning the audit

1. Scope: one manufacturing process (line, cell or process step chain) for one product family, from material receipt at the process to the hand-over of finished parts.
2. Criteria: list every document with its revision: control plan, PFMEA, process flow, work and inspection instructions, the customer-specific requirements, and the method (for example VDA 6.3 element P6) if the customer requires one.
3. Shifts: plan time on every shift on which the process runs, and be present for at least one shift handover.
4. Auditor: qualified for process audits (7.2.3) and not responsible for the process audited (ISO 19011 definition of an internal audit).
5. Time: set by local policy. Rule of thumb: half a day to a day per process for a full audit; less for a follow-up audit of earlier findings.
6. Tell the area owner the scope, criteria and timing beforehand (rule of thumb; unannounced audits are set by local policy).

## Building the checklist

Use specific checklists for each audit (9.2.2.1 note). Build them from:

- **The control plan.** Pick the special characteristics and the high-risk rows. For each one: is the check done at the stated frequency and sample, with the stated gauge, recorded as stated, and is the reaction plan followed when a result is out?
- **The PFMEA.** For the causes with the highest action priority, is the prevention and detection control the PFMEA claims actually in place at the station, and in the control plan?
- **Process inputs and resources (turtle view).** Material identification and traceability, machines and tools (maintenance, tool life), people (qualification matrix, training records), methods (work instructions at the station, current revision), measurement (calibration and capability study), environment (order and cleanliness).
- **Starts and changes.** Set-up and first-off release, restart after a stop, temporary alternate methods, engineering changes.
- **Nonconforming product.** Identification and segregation, rework to instruction, disposition.
- **History.** Customer complaints, internal scrap trends, open findings of the last audit.

Rule of thumb: 20–40 questions for a full process audit, so that each can be checked on evidence in the time available.

## Collecting evidence

- **Trace one row both ways.** Take one control-plan row and follow it to the station: instruction, gauge, record, reaction. Then take one station record and trace it back to its control-plan row and PFMEA line. A check with no row, or a row with no check, is a finding.
- **Sample records.** Rule of thumb: look at the records of several recent days and of every shift, not only today's. A gap that appears on one shift only is still a gap.
- **Watch the work** against the instruction for at least one full cycle (rule of thumb), and ask the operator how they know the result is good and what they do when it is not.
- **Write down the evidence as you see it:** document number and revision, record date and shift, gauge ID, setting value, station. Evidence that cannot be named is not evidence.
- **Product at risk.** When the evidence shows product may be nonconforming, tell the area owner at once so that it is contained; the audit report states that this was done.

## VDA 6.3 scoring

As checked in November 2026: VDA 6.3 4th revised edition, January 2023. The volume is a paid document; the points below come from VDA QMC's public interpretation sheet and secondary summaries, so check the current text and the customer requirements before you rate.

- **Questions.** 59 questions in the 2023 edition, 17 of them starred (*) as higher-risk questions (en-standard.eu). Series production is element P6; the whole set runs P1 to P7 (certivo).
- **Points.** Each rated question gets 10, 8, 6, 4 or 0 points; no other values (DQS, mobile2b).
- **2/3 rule.** An element, sub-element or process step in which fewer than 2/3 of the questions are rated is left out of the overall result; its rated questions become "not rated", no percentage is shown for it, and the downgrading rules are not applied to it. With several process steps, count all questions of all steps per sub-element (VDA QMC SI).
- **Fulfilment percentage.** The usual reading: points achieved ÷ (questions rated × 10) × 100 (mobile2b). For P6 with several process steps, apply the edition's own rule for combining steps.
- **Classes.** A ≥ 90 %, B 80 % to < 90 %, C < 80 % in a customer's supplier-evaluation document and a secondary summary (Grammer, certivo). Other summaries give different bands (mobile2b), so confirm them.
- **Downgrading.** The class is not set by the percentage alone. Published summaries agree that a question scored 0 lowers the class whatever the average (mobile2b, certivo). One OEM supplement adds: a starred question with 4 points or fewer, or any question at 0, rules out an A; a starred question at 0 gives a C (Formel Q Capability appendix). Apply the rule of the edition and the customer that the audit is for.

Worked example (invented round numbers): 20 questions rated, 18 at 10 points, 1 at 6 and 1 at 0. Fulfilment = (180 + 6 + 0) ÷ (20 × 10) × 100 = 186 ÷ 200 × 100 = 93 %. The percentage is in the A band, but a question at 0 triggers downgrading, so the result is not an A; the exact class follows the edition and the customer's rule.
