# Kata and A3

Two routines for topics where the way to the target is not known. Both are PDCA in practice; they differ in rhythm and in who does the work.

## Which one

| Use | When |
|---|---|
| Improvement kata | One learner (team leader, engineer) can run small, fast experiments in the area; a coach is available every day or nearly every day |
| A3 | One owner must analyse a problem, propose countermeasures and get agreement from several functions or a manager; review is weekly or at milestones |

If the topic is a defect whose cause must be proven with evidence, run structured problem solving first and come back with the cause.

## Improvement kata (Rother)

1. Understand the challenge: the longer-range goal (the starter pack gives about 3 to 24 months).
2. Grasp the current condition with numbers: run charts, cycle times, defect rates, observed on the floor.
3. Set the next target condition: a described state, measurable, with an achieve-by date, usually 1 to 4 weeks out (starter pack). It says what should be happening, not what to do, and is not "the lack of something".
4. Experiment toward it, one obstacle at a time, recording each step in an experimenting record: step, expected result, actual result, learning.

## Coaching cycle (order of the starter kata, in this skill's words)

The coach works through five points at the learner's storyboard:

1. Where should the process be by the next target date? The learner states the target condition.
2. Where is it today? The learner shows current numbers.
   - Then a look back at the last experiment: the planned step, the prediction written before it, the outcome, and the lesson.
3. What blocks the way? The learner names the obstacles and picks one to work on.
4. What experiment comes next, and what result does the learner predict?
5. When will coach and learner look at the result together?

- Clarifying checks: data instead of opinion; the learner shows it at the process; the prediction is written down before the step.
- Keep the cycle short: Rother gives at most about 20 minutes a day, and argues daily short cycles beat a long weekly session.
- When the learner reaches the limit of what is known, go straight to the next experiment.

## A3 (Shook)

One sheet, read left to right. The layout is flexible; the logic is fixed.

| Section | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Title, owner, date | What is this about; who owns it; latest revision |
| Background | Why does it matter to the business? |
| Current condition | Where do things stand, with facts and a picture? |
| Goal | What result is needed, by when? |
| Analysis | What causes the gap? Use the simplest tool that shows cause and effect |
| Countermeasures | What will close the gap, and how do they act on the causes? |
| Plan | Who does what, when; which indicator shows progress |
| Follow-up | How will we know it worked; what can go wrong; how is the learning shared |

- The A3 is a dialogue between the owner and a mentor, not a form to fill alone.
- A countermeasure that works ends in a new standard and a check that it is used, the same as any other format.
