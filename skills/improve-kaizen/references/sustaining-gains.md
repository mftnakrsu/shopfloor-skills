# Sustaining gains

Why gains fade, and how the new standard, its check and the 30/60/90 plan hold them.

## The principle

- No kaizen without a standard: once a gain is reached, update the standard so it does not disappear (Ohno, via LEI).
- Management keeps standards (SDCA) and raises them (PDCA); a gain that is not handed to SDCA decays (Imai, via secondary sources).
- Field evidence: post-event performance review and acceptance of the changes predicted results 9 to 18 months later (Glover).

## What a finished result contains

| Part | Content | Example (invented) |
|---|---|---|
| New standard | The document that now defines the work, its owner, release date, old version withdrawn | Revised standard work sheet for a packing station, owner the area supervisor |
| Training | Every shift and every operator who runs the area, recorded | 2 shifts, 12 operators, signed training list |
| Check | Who checks, how, how often | Team leader checks one cycle against the sheet each shift; supervisor adds one audit question weekly |
| Measured gain | Same metric and method as the baseline, over every shift | Rejects per 1,000 from 40 to 25 over 4 weeks |

## The 30/60/90 checkpoints

At each checkpoint review the same three items. The 30-day check is common event practice; 60 and 90 days are a rule of thumb. Dates and who attends are set by local policy.

| Checkpoint | Standard in use | Metric | Open actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days | Audit or check results; drift at about 30 days is the common failure (Manifest) | Against target, all shifts | Most closed; the rest re-dated with owner |
| 60 days | Checks still done and recorded | Holding at target | All closed or handed over |
| 90 days | Standard runs without the team; checks run by the line | Holding; confirm the gain | Topic closed, lessons recorded |

## When the gain slips

1. Go and see whether the standard is followed.
2. If it is not followed, ask why before retraining: is it workable on every shift, every product, with the material and tools there? Often the standard itself must change.
3. If it is followed and the metric still slips, the cause was not what the team thought: open a new PDCA turn or structured problem solving.
4. If the target is missed, open a corrective action with an owner, a cause analysis and a due date (Manifest).
