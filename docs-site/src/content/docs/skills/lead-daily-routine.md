Designs, runs and audits daily shopfloor management: the tier meeting cascade (Regelkommunikation), the SQDCP board, same-day escalation with a reply path, action follow-up and routine adherence.

## Three modes

The skill picks the mode from your request.

| Mode | You give | You get |
|---|---|---|
| Design | The leadership levels and who attends, at least one shift start time, the KPIs you track | A tier cascade with times, timeboxes, attendees and agenda; a board layout; escalation rules; the KPI gaps by SQDCP category; open questions |
| Run | Today's board values and open actions | A timed agenda for today's meeting, with the action and escalation lists |
| Audit | Observations of real meetings, or a description of the routine | A scored self-audit with the three biggest gaps, or each gap with its fix, a corrected agenda and the adherence metrics to track |

If a design input is missing, the skill asks for it in a short list and stops. It does not draft a schedule on invented facts. Missing targets or later shift times do not block a design; they are marked "set by local policy" or "assumption" and listed as open questions.

## Rules it applies

- One fixed agenda at every tier: safety, quality, delivery, cost, people, then actions, new problems and escalations. Only the reds are discussed.
- Every red gets a countermeasure with an owner and a date, or an escalation.
- An escalation is a request for support, never blame. Its answer returns to the raising team the same day.
- Problem solving happens outside the meeting. The meeting starts, tracks and closes the work.
- Timeboxes, KPI counts and tier timing are marked as rules of thumb, with their sources in the skill's reference files.

## What is in the folder

- `SKILL.md`: the procedure.
- `references/`: tier design, board design and the routine audit metrics, each with its sources.
- `assets/`: templates for the tier cascade, the tier board and the routine self-audit.
